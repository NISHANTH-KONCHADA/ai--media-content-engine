## 3. Qualitative Feedback Analysis

### Key Themes Favoring Variant B (59.1% Preference):
1. **Statistic Anchors Credibility:** The *"87% of podcast clips fail in the first 3 seconds"* claim created a concrete curiosity gap that participants found more attention-grabbing than the problem statement.
2. **Set-Up Feels More Complete:** Multiple respondents described Card A as *"abrupt"* or *"cut off"* without the contextual lead-in Card B provides.
3. **Cleaner Scan:** Several respondents preferred B's *"cleaner visual hierarchy"* and *"less text clutter"* for fast feed scrolling.

### Quotes from Verified Responses:
- *P01 (YouTube Educator, 1-2 yrs):* "The statistic '87% of podcast clips fail in the first 3 seconds' creates curiosity and makes the audience want to know what the one change is. It also gives the content a sense of credibility and urgency."
- *P06 (Video Editor, 5+ yrs):* "Viewers scrolling through short-form feeds decide within seconds whether to keep watching. Leading with immediate value earns their attention first."
- *P20 (Content Agency Lead, 3-5 yrs):* "Card B feels more direct and punchy for a quick scroll stop, focusing on a cleaner visual hierarchy and immediate framing."
- *P19 (Video Editor, 1-2 yrs) [dissenting, voted A]:* "Card A hooks attention immediately with a concrete time cost ('Stop wasting 6 hours clipping podcast audio') and pairs it with a clear, counterintuitive rule."

## 4. Strategic Implications & Honest Reconciliation

The hypothesis in `test_plan.md` predicted the problem-led hook (Variant A) would win. **It did not** — the curiosity/metric hook was preferred by this sample. We report this divergence rather than hide it:

1. The 30 shipped campaign assets use action-oriented problem hooks (Variant A framework), which the dissenting voters still found effective — A was preferred by 40.9%, not rejected.
2. Recommended next iteration: produce a Variant-B-style batch (metric/curiosity-led headlines) and run a live A/B comparison in the first posting week, as proposed in the launch kit.
3. With p = 0.2617, we do **not** claim a validated winner; the directional signal favors metric-led hooks for this audience.

---

## 5. Limitations

1. **Non-randomized card order:** Cards were shown in fixed A-then-B order in the form; order effects were not controlled.
2. **Role ambiguity:** 2 respondents self-identified as "Other" creator-adjacent roles rather than a named creator/editor category.
3. **Self-reported roles:** Participant roles and experience are self-declared via the form; no identity verification was performed.
4. **Single stimulus pair:** Only one hook pair was tested; results may not generalize across topics or formats.
