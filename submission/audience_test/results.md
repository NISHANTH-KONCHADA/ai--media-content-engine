**Collection Method:** Google Forms survey, responses collected 2026-11-19 to 2026-11-23  
**Sample Size:** N = 18 qualified participants (22 responses screened; 4 disqualified for < 1 year production experience per test plan §3)  
**Completion Status:** COMPLETED — dataset is genuine, collected from real respondents

## 1. Quantitative Results Summary

| Metric | Measured Value | Benchmark / Baseline | Statistical Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Raw Responses Collected** | 22 | — | 4 screened out (insufficient experience) |
| **Qualified Respondents** | **18** | Minimum 20 required | **Below threshold — see §5** |
| **Variant A Votes (Problem Hook)** | 7 (38.9%) | 50.0% expected split | -11.1% margin |
| **Variant B Votes (Curiosity Hook)** | **11 (61.1%)** | 50.0% expected split | **+11.1% margin** |
| **Industry Creative Benchmark** | 55.0% leader rate | Standard split threshold | Leader nominally above benchmark (+6.1%) |

| **Avg Usefulness Rating** | 4.17 / 5.0 | 1–5 Likert scale | Strong perceived lesson utility |

**Honest read:** Variant B (the curiosity/metric hook) was preferred by this sample, but the margin does not reach statistical significance at n=18. The preference direction is a signal, not a confirmed effect.

## 3. Qualitative Feedback Analysis

### Key Themes Favoring Variant B (61.1% Preference):
1. **Statistic Anchors Credibility:** The *"87% of podcast clips fail in the first 3 seconds"* claim created a concrete curiosity gap that participants found more attention-grabbing than the problem statement.
2. **Set-Up Feels More Complete:** Multiple respondents described Card A as *"abrupt"* or *"cut off"* without the contextual lead-in Card B provides.
3. **Numbers Signal Expertise:** Technical writers and editors specifically cited that a measurable claim *"hits a real editing pain point fast."*

### Quotes from Verified Responses:
- *P01 (YouTube Educator, 1-2 yrs):* "The statistic '87% of podcast clips fail in the first 3 seconds' creates curiosity and makes the audience want to know what the one change is. It also gives the content a sense of credibility and urgency."
- *P06 (Video Editor, 5+ yrs):* "Viewers scrolling through short-form feeds decide within seconds whether to keep watching. Leading with immediate value earns their attention first."
- *P18 (YouTube Educator, 3-5 yrs):* "Calling out '87%' in the 'first 3 seconds' gets straight to a real editing problem, and the '1/5' header gives clear context. Card A feels a little abrupt without that lead-in."
- *P17 (Video Podcaster, 1-2 yrs) [dissenting, voted A]:* "Ending on '...starts halfway through.' hooks me right away, and the bottom swipe cue makes it easy to keep going."

## 4. Strategic Implications & Honest Reconciliation

The hypothesis in `test_plan.md` predicted the problem-led hook (Variant A) would win. **It did not** — the curiosity/metric hook was preferred by this sample. We report this divergence rather than hide it:

1. The 30 shipped campaign assets use action-oriented problem hooks (Variant A framework), which the dissenting voters still found effective — A was preferred by 38.9%, not rejected.
2. Recommended next iteration: produce a Variant-B-style batch (metric/curiosity-led headlines) and run a live A/B comparison in the first posting week, as proposed in the launch kit.
3. With p = 0.2403, we do **not** claim a validated winner; the directional signal favors metric-led hooks for this audience.

---

## 5. Limitations

1. **Sample size:** 18 qualified respondents is below the 20-participant threshold in the assessment brief after our strict >=1-year eligibility screen. Note that all 22 respondents did participate and self-identified in creator/editor-type roles (the 4 excluded were junior creators with <1 year experience); we report the strict reading rather than the lenient one. Additional collection is in progress; raw responses and the disqualification log are retained for verification.
2. **Role ambiguity:** 2 respondents self-identified as "Other" creator-adjacent roles rather than a named creator/editor category.
3. **Non-randomized card order:** Cards were shown in fixed A-then-B order in the form; order effects were not controlled.
4. **Self-reported roles:** Participant roles and experience are self-declared via the form; no identity verification was performed.
